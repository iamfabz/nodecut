# Story — structure for films that have to explain or persuade

A 20-second ad can get by on a mood. Anything longer, or anything that has to teach or convince, needs a shape. These are
the shapes that work, and the tests that tell you whether yours does.

## The arc

Three moves, in this order:

1. **Setup** — where we are, and what is at stake. Short. The viewer needs to know what they are watching within two
   seconds.
2. **Turn** — the thing that is not what you expected. This is the beat the film exists for. If there is no turn, the film
   is a list.
3. **Landing** — where it resolves. The last line or image should be the one remembered, so it must be the plainest.

Kurzgesagt's production notes put a new concept at most every 30–45 seconds in long-form; in a 30-second film that means
**one idea, not three**. If you find yourself with two turns, you have two films.

## The "but / therefore" test

Write the beats as sentences joined by *and then*. Then rewrite them with **but** and **therefore**.

> We see a cow *and then* we see a jar *and then* we see a chart.

That is a list, and it feels like one. Now:

> We expect a field and a cow — **but** this is a seasoning, packaged like a fragrance — **therefore** the cow is a
> customer, not a joke.

If no beat can be joined by *but* or *therefore*, you have no story, only a sequence. Two of the three joints should be one
of those words.

## The hook

The first beat decides everything. Four that work:

| hook | how it opens | when to use |
|---|---|---|
| **the interrupt** | something visually unexpected, no context | social, where attention is cheapest to lose |
| **the claim** | the boldest true statement, straight away | when the claim is genuinely surprising |
| **the question** | a question the viewer wants answered | explainers |
| **the world** | a beautiful place, held, with nothing explained yet | brand films, where mood is the message |

What does not work: a logo, a title card, an intro, a build-up. Nothing is earned yet.

## The misconception-first shape

The strongest structure for teaching: state what people *think* is true, then show why it is not, then give the real
model.

```
"Most people imagine X."
"It isn't X — here's the thing that breaks it."
"It's actually Y."
```

It works because the viewer spends the film correcting a belief they already hold, which is far stickier than learning
something new from nothing.

## The guided-discovery shape

3Blue1Brown's technique: do not state the conclusion, arrive at it. Show the two cases, let the pattern appear, then name
it. Slower, but the viewer owns the idea instead of receiving it.

Use it when the idea is worth remembering. Do not use it in a 15-second ad — there is no time to discover anything.

## Mayer's principles, applied

Richard Mayer's research on multimedia learning reduces to a few rules that survive contact with short video:

| principle | what it means on a timeline |
|---|---|
| **multimedia** | words *and* pictures beat words alone, when they say different things |
| **segmenting** | let the viewer take one idea at a time; one beat, one idea |
| **signalling** | show what matters — an arrow, a highlight, a colour change, not a caption saying "this matters" |
| **redundancy** | do not put the narration on screen word for word; the eye and ear compete |
| **spatial contiguity** | put the label next to the thing, not in a corner with a line to it |
| **temporal contiguity** | the word and the image arrive together, not one after the other |

The redundancy one is where most explainer films fail: identical text and voice-over is a double load, not emphasis.

## Choosing a shape by length

| length | shape |
|---|---|
| under 15 s | hook → landing. No turn; the hook *is* the turn |
| 20–30 s | setup → turn → landing |
| 45–60 s | misconception-first, or guided discovery |
| 90 s+ | two turns minimum, and a pattern interrupt at ~45 s |

## The last line

Write it first. If you know what the final frame says, everything before it has a direction to point in. Films written
front-to-back usually end on a line that could have been third.
