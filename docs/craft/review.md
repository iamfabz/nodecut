# Review — scoring your own work before the user sees it

The user should never be the first person to notice that a beat is dead. This is the loop that catches it, and it costs a
contact sheet and five honest minutes.

## The loop

1. **Capture** — take a contact sheet at every beat's midpoint and at the moments that matter (a reveal, a turn, the end
   card). `hub_contact_sheet` gives one labelled grid in about a second.
2. **Look at the images.** Not at the code, and not from memory of what you intended.
3. **Score** each beat 1–10 on the axes below.
4. **Write down the three worst problems**, specifically. "Beat 4: the ring sits on the jar and reads as a crop" is a
   problem. "Beat 4 needs work" is not.
5. **Fix them**, take another sheet.
6. **Stop** at 8+ on every axis, or after three rounds. A fourth round rarely improves anything and always costs an hour.

## The axes

Score 1–10 on each:

| axis | the question |
|---|---|
| **composition** | is there one clear thing to look at, with the rest receding? |
| **motion** | does the beat change for its whole length, or die halfway? |
| **typography** | can every line be read at 640 px wide, on the frame it actually sits on? |
| **payoff** | is something different at the end of the beat than at the start? |
| **polish** | is anything clipped, overlapping, misaligned, or a placeholder? |
| **sync** (films with sound) | does each landing coincide with the music or voice? |

## What usually scores low

Ordered by how often they turn up:

1. **A beat that dies halfway.** The type finishes at 1.5 s and the beat runs to 3.3 s with nothing happening. Add a slow
   push, a moving highlight, a counter.
2. **Copy that is still assembling when the cut lands.** The line never completed. Time the arrival and hold.
3. **Text on a busy area with no separation.** Contrast measured on the actual frame, not against black.
4. **Everything entering the same way.** Every beat fades in. Rotate the entrances.
5. **Dead corners.** Subject centred, nothing else in the frame. Add furniture.
6. **The same medium three beats running.** The checker refuses this, but it also simply reads badly.
7. **A graphic sitting on top of the subject.** A ring over a jar reads as a crop. Keep overlays outside the silhouette.
8. **A chart with no numbers, or a diagram with no labels.** Drawn is not designed.
9. **A world that looks like a different film** — palette or type not from `frame.md`.
10. **An empty final beat.** The landing should be the most resolved frame in the film, not the emptiest.

## Reviewing the review

Two cheap tests that catch things scoring misses:

**The mute test.** Watch with sound off. If the story survives, the picture is doing its job. If it only makes sense with
the voice, the visuals are decoration.

**The squint test.** Squint at a still. If the hierarchy collapses into one grey field, nothing is dominant.

**The stranger test.** Show one frame out of context. Can someone say what the film is about? If not, the frame is mood,
not message.

## Writing the three problems

A problem you cannot act on is not a finding. Each one gets:

- **where** — the beat and the timestamp,
- **what is wrong** — visible in the image,
- **what to do** — a concrete change, not a direction.

> Beat 4, 0:11.2 — the hairline measure crosses the jar at its widest point, so the jar looks cut in half. Move the measure
> above the lid and the ring out to 400 px so both sit outside the silhouette.

That is a fix. Anything vaguer will come back as a note from the user.
