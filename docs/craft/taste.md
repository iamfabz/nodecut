# Taste — committing to a look, and banning the rest

A film that has not chosen a look gets one by default, and the default is the same for everyone: centred text, a soft
gradient, everything fading in. The fix is not more decisions — it is **fewer, made earlier, and written down.**

## The design read

Before a palette, before a font, say what the film is *supposed to feel like*, and why. One paragraph, specific, tied to the
audience and the promise:

> Premium expert explainer: calm authority, high trust, low ornament. The viewer should feel they are being told something
> true by someone who does not need to raise their voice.

> Youth science short: bright, curious, kinetic. Make the invisible feel tactile. Nothing should sit still for more than a
> second.

A weak read is where the rest of the film goes wrong: "modern and clean" is not a read, it is an absence of one.

## Three dials

Set these before you design anything. They stop the film drifting:

| dial | low | mid | high |
|---|---|---|---|
| **visual variance** | one tight system, repeated grammar | scene families, each with a look | every beat a different visual mode |
| **motion intensity** | calm holds, small transitions | clear accents and reveals | fast kinetic language, frequent direction changes |
| **information density** | one idea per frame | idea + support detail | layered diagrams, callouts, numbers |

A 20-second luxury ad might be variance 2, motion 3, density 2. A social explainer might be 4, 5, 4. Write the three
numbers in `frame.md` and hold to them.

## One look, written down

`frame.md` in the project holds:

- **palette** — 4–6 hex values, each with a role, never pure black or pure white
- **type** — two families, each with a job, and the sizes you will actually use
- **materials** — what the film is *made of*: glass, paper, ink, light, grain, chrome
- **motion register** — the entrance habit (snappy expo, springy overshoot, slow float, hard cuts)
- **the ban list** — see below

## The ban list

The part most people skip, and the part that makes films look different from each other.

Ban, explicitly:

1. **the generic AI look** — purple/blue gradients, glassmorphism cards, Inter as a display face, everything fading in at the
   same speed, centred text stacks on nothing;
2. **every look already used in this project folder** — open the other films and read their `frame.md`. If a film there is
   "warm amber, wide-tracked Helvetica, dark", then this one cannot be. Reusing a family is fine; reusing palette + type +
   signature technique is not;
3. **anything the user said they are tired of.**

A good ban list names hexes, not vibes: *"no `#6C63FF`–`#4A00E0` gradients, no white cards with blur, no Inter Display."*

## What actually makes a look feel designed

- **One accent, used rarely.** An accent that appears on every beat is the base colour.
- **A material, not a style.** "Backlit amber glass" produces decisions. "Clean and modern" produces nothing.
- **Consistency of grade.** One grade over the whole film, including the graphics and the labels, so the furniture belongs
  to the same lens.
- **Deliberate imperfection.** Grain, a slight vignette, a 4-frame burn on the cuts. Perfect frames look synthetic.
- **Something in the corners.** A section label, a timecode, a frame counter. It fills dead space and reads as designed.

## Filling the frame

A frame with a subject and nothing else looks like a template. Give every frame a second layer:

| layer | example |
|---|---|
| a rule or measure | hairlines that open from the centre, a baseline under a chart |
| an index | `III · THE ELEMENT`, `03 / 06` |
| a gauge | an arc that draws around the product and keeps turning slowly |
| a counter | a number that climbs |
| light | a warm pool behind the subject, a rim that travels |

Two of these per beat is plenty. Five is noise.

## The test

Hold the finished frame at arm's length and squint. If you can see the hierarchy — one thing dominant, one supporting, the
furniture receding — the composition works. If everything is the same weight, nothing is designed.
